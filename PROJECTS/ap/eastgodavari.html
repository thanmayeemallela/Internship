East Godavari District

East Godavari is famous for Konaseema, Godavari river, temples, agriculture,and natural beauty.
East Godavari is a prominent and culturally rich district in Andhra Pradesh, headquartered in the historic city of Rajamahendravaram (Rajahmundry).
Renowned as the "Cultural Capital of Andhra Pradesh", it serves as the fertile lifeline of the Godavari river delta and is celebrated for its rich heritage, agriculture, and spiritual significance.

History & Speciality

History

Ancient Dynasties:
The region’s history dates back to the Nanda and Mauryan empires. It was subsequently ruled by prominent South Indian dynasties, including the Satavahanas, Eastern Chalukyas, Kakatiyas, and the Reddy kings.

British Era & Bifurcation:
The British established the Rajahmundry district in 1823. In 1925, this massive territory was officially divided into East Godavari (headquartered in Kakinada) and West Godavari.

Recent Reorganization:
Following a sweeping state reorganization, the district was restructured in 2022, and Rajamahendravaram (formerly Rajahmundry) now serves as its headquarters.



Specialities

Cultural Capital: Often referred to as the cultural capital of Andhra Pradesh, Rajamahendravaram is the birthplace of the modern Telugu language and literature. It was home to the legendary Adikavi Nannayya, who translated the Mahabharata into Telugu.

Divine Tourism: The district is home to highly revered pilgrimage sites, including the famous Annavaram Temple and the ancient Shiva temple at Draksharamam, considered one of the Pancharamas.
